# Editorial and research guide

## Murali Sugumar's voice

Audience may include business owners, students, designers, and developers. Infer the relevant group from the topic. Default to professional but approachable English, including readers who use English as a second language.

- Prefer familiar words: use, help, show, check, build.
- Keep one main idea per paragraph. Split difficult sentences; vary sentence length naturally. Do not impose a mechanical readability score or sentence-length quota.
- Explain acronyms and technical concepts near their first use. A short example often explains more than a formal definition.
- Avoid idioms, sarcasm, dramatic fragments, unnecessary jargon, exaggerated claims, and stock openings such as “In today's fast-paced digital world.”
- Use headings that tell readers what the section answers. Do not force keywords into them.
- Use lists for steps or parallel items, tables for real comparisons, and diagrams when they explain a process more clearly than prose. Use neither as decoration.
- Close with a useful action when appropriate. Service links should follow naturally from the topic, not turn every educational article into a sales pitch.

## Evidence and originality

Research primary documentation, official project repositories, original studies, and firsthand material supplied by Murali. Secondary sources can help with context or perspectives, but distinguish them from original evidence. Check that cited pages support the exact claims made.

For each important factual claim, track its source, date/version when relevant, and any qualification. Keep this evidence ledger in task scratch files for a substantial researched article; a short edit does not need a new research dossier.

- Describe a skill from its actual current instructions, not its marketing name alone.
- Identify the exact project when names overlap. Distinguish a skill, a plugin, a library, and a hosted service.
- Treat authors' self-reported performance claims as claims, not independently confirmed results.
- Do not use fabricated sample numbers, names, quotes, or anecdotes from upstream skill examples.
- Support “best” recommendations with explicit criteria and meaningful tradeoffs. Distinguish instruction review from hands-on testing.
- If evidence is unavailable, remove or qualify the claim. Ask for necessary firsthand material instead of inventing it.
- Do not pad a summary of sources. Add a practical example, decision guide, comparison, or explanation suited to the reader.
- Existing instructions or past installations prove only what was actually observed. Installation does not establish article quality, SEO results, or client outcomes.

## Tutorials and technical posts

State prerequisites, version context, steps, expected result, and meaningful limitations where relevant. Test code and instructions where possible without unauthorized external effects. Keep essential setup in examples; do not claim unrun code was tested. Include troubleshooting only for plausible problems in the actual workflow.

A hypothetical business example should be labelled as such. A first-person case study needs Murali's actual evidence and role. Never add a personal discovery story merely to make an opening sound authentic.

## Editing existing work

Preserve the author's position, useful wording, and scope. Improve what is unclear, unsupported, repetitive, or difficult to follow. Check live URLs and retained claims when changes depend on their accuracy. If the user asks for a full rewrite, reorganize as needed; otherwise avoid rewriting untouched sections.

## Final editorial pass

Read for the intended audience. Check the main answer is easy to find, examples support it, sources match claims, and the writing sounds natural. Remove repeated conclusions and formulaic sections. Confirm any claimed human review actually happened. Separate editor-only notes from publishable text so unresolved notes cannot be mistaken for finished copy.


## Evidence of added value

Before drafting a substantial article, identify a concrete reader benefit beyond summarising other pages: a decision table, worked example, tested procedure, original screenshot, clear explanation of a difficult concept, or Murali's supplied project experience. Match the contribution to the topic; do not require firsthand access the user does not have. If the article has no useful difference, narrow the question or strengthen the explanation rather than making it longer.

Respect source authorship and image rights. Paraphrase with attribution, keep quotations short and accurate, and do not rewrite a competitor paragraph by paragraph. Credit screenshots and supplied assets appropriately. Disclose a material affiliate or sponsor relationship if one is known; do not assume every external link is sponsored.

If financial, medical, legal, or other high-consequence topics arise, use current authoritative evidence, preserve important qualifications, and identify when expert review is needed. Keep claimed expertise accurate.

## AI assistance and human review

AI assistance does not itself prove quality or disqualify content. Research and review must establish accuracy, relevance and a useful contribution. Do not generate many near-identical keyword pages or city variations as a substitute for useful content. AI detection scores and promises of “undetectable” writing are not quality checks.

Recommend human editorial review before publication, particularly for personal experience and sensitive claims. This recommendation does not create an extra approval gate when publication is already explicitly authorized. If relevant, suggest a truthful short explanation of how AI was used. Do not claim an AI disclosure is a universal Google ranking requirement or assert Murali has reviewed a draft without evidence.

## Completion checks

For a full article, assess these decisions rather than counting keywords:

- Can the intended reader find a clear answer and take the promised next step?
- Does the article add a useful explanation, example, evidence, or decision aid?
- Are important claims supported, current where needed, and consistent with their sources?
- Are terms explained and sentences understandable without specialist English?
- Are personal experience, hypothetical examples and independent testing correctly distinguished?
- Does the handoff contain only resolved publishable content, with editorial questions kept separately?

Fix material defects within the authorized task. If a missing fact blocks accuracy, narrow the claim or report it as pending; do not hide it in finished copy. Never label a file ready to publish while leaving invented links, unsupported claims, or unresolved placeholders in it.
